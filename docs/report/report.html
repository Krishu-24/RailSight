Executive summary

A camera that knows where the train is going

Most railway deaths in India are not the train accidents that make headlines: they are people and animals on the track. A train cannot steer, and needs hundreds of metres to stop. RailSight is an academic prototype that watches the line ahead from a single forward camera and, for every hazard, decides whether it is in the train's path and whether there is still distance to stop.

What we built

  • Rail-scene segmentation (SegFormer-B2, fine-tuned on RailSem19) that finds the ego track and a metric danger corridor as wide as the train.
  • Distance from the rail gauge: the rails are a ruler of known width, so every image row gets a pixels-per-metre scale without a depth sensor.
  • Obstacle detection (YOLO11m, fine-tuned) for people, animals, vehicles, rocks, fallen vegetation, debris and other rail vehicles, plus a class-agnostic check for unknown objects on the track.
  • A transparent risk engine (braking physics, in-path prediction, hysteresis) giving SAFE / WARNING / CRITICAL, an interactive demo and a command-line tool.

What we learned

Test where the system will be used.Detectors fine-tuned on public "railway obstacle" images scored 0.67–0.71 mAP50 on their own test set, but under 0.08 on real cab-view frames — worse than an un-tuned COCO model. Mining ~16k boxes from RailSem19's dense masks fixed it (0.48) without losing accuracy elsewhere.

Equally important were the things that did not work, and the measurements that showed it: a motion-based mask for the train's own dashboard, a rail-pair gauge measurement, a training time budget that silently broke the learning-rate schedule. They are covered in section 7.

01 · The problem

The danger is on the track, not in the accident statistics

India's Ministry of Railways recorded 40 consequential train accidents in 2023-24, down from 473 in 2000-01 [1]. The National Crime Records Bureau counted 21,803 railway deaths in 2023, 73% of them from falls or people struck on the track [2]; add 27,489 cattle run-over incidents in 2022 [3] and 79 elephants killed in five years [4]. Worldwide, trespassers and level-crossing users account for 96% of rail fatalities in UIC data [5].

Why the driver alone is not enough

A passenger train at 100 km/h needs roughly 440 m to stop in our model; engineers quote 700 m to 1 km for express trains [6]. Night, fog, curves and long monotonous shifts make spotting a hazard that far ahead harder still. A system that never tires helps even when a stop is impossible: braking a few seconds earlier cuts impact energy (it grows with the square of speed) and an earlier horn gives people and animals time to clear the line.

02 · System design

Two questions for every hazard

1 · Is it in the path?

The ego track is the connected track region that starts nearest the bottom centre of the view. It is traced row by row upwards (at switches, following the branch closest to the previous row), and two robust fits summarise it: the centre line (quadratic, for curves) and the track width (linear in the image row, because on flat ground the projected gauge shrinks linearly to zero at the horizon). The danger corridor is the ego track widened to the train's envelope (3.2 m + 0.5 m each side). An object is in the path if it is inside the corridor now, or its lateral motion carries it inside within ±2 s of the train's arrival.

2 · Can the train still stop?

The width fit gives a pixels-per-metre scale for every row. With the gauge G (1.435 m standard, 1.676 m Indian broad gauge) and focal length f, an object touching the ground at row y is Z = f · G / width(y) away — no depth sensor, no stereo. Its distance is compared with the stopping distance v·t + v² / 2a plus a 50 m margin: in path and within stopping distance is CRITICAL, in path beyond it or close to the corridor is WARNING. A hysteresis state machine (3 frames to raise, 15 to lower) stops the alert from flickering.

Unknown objects

A detector only finds classes it was trained on. The segmentation model, however, knows what a normal track bed looks like (rails, sleepers, ballast, grass), so any non-track pixels on the track itself are flagged as an unknown object, sized physically through the gauge scale (≥ 0.3 m wide) and confirmed over three segmentation updates. When the model is unsure of the track itself (darkness, glare, blur) the check switches off and the display says so, rather than inventing hazards.

Why rules, not another network, for the decision?No dataset labels "this frame was dangerous", and a safety decision should be explainable. Learning handles perception; transparent physics decides.

Audit of the obstacle datasets

Before training, every source was inspected visually and statistically. Two problems would have silently corrupted the results:

  • 74% of one dataset's boxes were "Plant" — weeds between sleepers photographed top-down by an inspection camera, not obstacles in front of a train. Plants, standing trees and "flood" were dropped; the remaining classes were mapped onto six hazards (person, animal, vehicle, rock, vegetation, obstacle).
  • The published splits leaked: Roboflow stores three augmented copies of each training image, and the sources contain consecutive video frames, so near-identical images sat in both train and test. Images were grouped by source and by perceptual hash (dHash) into 1,825 groups and re-split 80/10/10 by group, stratified on the rarest class. Test images carry no augmented copies.

Mining in-domain labels from RailSem19 (detection v2)

RailSem19's masks label every human, car, truck and rail-vehicle pixel in 8,500 real cab-view frames. Their connected components become boxes — about 16,400 training boxes (4,237 person, 8,828 vehicle, 3,289 of a new rail_vehicle class: another train on or beside the line). Visual checks showed the masks' noise: "vehicles" on billboards and camera watermarks, "rail vehicles" on catenary gantries. Filters were added for each: minimum size, minimum fill ratio (thin lattices fail it), no small boxes on the image border, and rail vehicles must stand on track. The segmentation split was kept, so RailSem19 test frames stay unseen by every model.

Licence boundaryRailSem19 frames may not leave the local machine, so every model that uses them was trained on the laptop, never on Kaggle, and only aggregate results are published.

All models start from public pretrained weights (SegFormer on Cityscapes, DeepLabV3 and the detectors on COCO). Segmentation splits RailSem19 80/10/10 with a fixed seed (6,800 / 850 / 850 frames, resized to 1280×720 once so that JPEG decoding does not bottleneck training).

Compute

Laptop: RTX 5060 Laptop GPU (8 GB, Blackwell), PyTorch 2.11 with CUDA 12.8 (required for RTX 50-series), run unattended overnight with a self-healing queue: each step resumes from its checkpoint and is retried, so a crash, an out-of-memory error or a PC restart cost minutes, not runs.

Kaggle: free 2× Tesla T4 GPUs (30 GPU-hours a week) trained the two larger v1 detectors in parallel with the laptop. Only the Roboflow-derived data (MIT / CC BY) was uploaded; a marker file kept the laptop queue from training the same models twice.

Fairness of comparisonsModels of one kind share data, splits, schedule and evaluator. Detection metrics are computed by one framework-independent evaluator for every model (including mAP50-95), with Ultralytics' own validator as a cross-check where class lists match.

The standard metric is not the one that mattersDeepLabV3 ties SegFormer-B2 on track IoU, yet its distances are about 20% worse: pixel overlap does not capture how cleanly the track edges follow the rails far away, which is exactly what ranging depends on. SegFormer-B2 is the default.

Ranging without a depth sensor or calibration

OSDaR23's lidar was the planned ground truth, but its server stayed down. Instead, every person in RailSem19 gives a second, independent distance from body height (f · 1.7 m / height_px). The focal length cancels in the ratio of the two estimates, so the check needs no calibration.

06 · On real video

What test sets do not show

Seven free cab-ride clips — open country, a level crossing, fog, a station and tunnel, two in-cab cameras and an Indian Railways line with people crossing — were run through the full pipeline. Rendering them revealed failure modes no test set contains.

Hunting false alarms

The first render with the final models raised CRITICAL far too often on some clips. Each cause got its own fix: the corridor now ends where the real view of the track ends, the train's own parts can be masked once per camera mounting (dashboards, hoods, the side of a coach), the unknown-object check switches off when the model is unsure of the track, and an unknown object must persist for three segmentation updates before it counts.

07 · The journey

Trial and error

The final numbers hide most of the work. These are the problems that changed the project, what we did, and what each taught us.

1

Silent GPU memory spill fixed

ProblemSegFormer-B2 at batch 8 needed 13.5 GB on an 8 GB card. Windows did not raise an error; it spilled into system RAM and training ran ~130× slower (an estimated 250 h). Full-resolution evaluation did the same (1 h 40 min per evaluation).

FixBenchmarked settings (batch 4 fits at 6.9 GB), evaluated one image at a time, added a guard that aborts with a clear message when an iteration is suspiciously slow.

2

Crashes, restarts and a fragile queue fixed

ProblemAn evaluation "on the CPU" still opened a GPU context and crashed training; later the PC restarted mid-run, and a background app caused two out-of-memory errors.

FixCheckpoints with full optimiser state, resume flags and a queue that retries each step. Each incident afterwards cost minutes, not runs. Evaluations during training now hide the GPU entirely.

3

A dataset that was mostly weeds, splits that leaked fixed

ProblemAudit before training: 74% of one set's boxes were weeds, and near-identical images sat in train and test.

FixDropped non-hazard classes, grouped duplicates by perceptual hash, re-split by group.

4

A training "safety" budget that broke the schedule fixed

ProblemOn Kaggle, Ultralytics' time= budget re-planned the epoch count to fill 10.5 h, so the learning rate never decayed. YOLO11m then scored below YOLO11s — but retraining correctly showed that was not the cause: the two models simply swap places between validation and test, within the noise of 270-image sets.

LessonCheck training curves, not only final scores; do not over-read small differences.

5

Great test scores, poor real-world detection fixed

ProblemAll three v1 detectors scored under 0.08 mAP50 on real cab-view frames — below an un-tuned COCO model — despite 0.67–0.71 on their own test split.

FixMined ~16k in-domain boxes from RailSem19's masks, filtered visually checked noise, added a rail-vehicle class: 0.43, then 0.48 at 960 px.

6

False alarms on real video fixed

ProblemThe model labelled a cab's dashboard as track; darkness produced phantom objects; single frames flickered.

FixCorridor limited to rows where track is visible, per-camera ignore regions, a low-confidence guard, three-update persistence for unknown objects. In-cab clip: 85% → 24% of time in CRITICAL.

7

Ideas that were measured and dropped

dropped Motion-based vehicle mask — pixels that stay still while moving should be the train itself, but rails straight ahead are just as still, so it removed the track.

optional Rail-pair gauge — measuring between the two rails fixed merged tracks at switches but picked wrong pairs elsewhere; it stays off by default.

dropped Low-confidence pixels as "unknown" — quadrupled false alarms on class boundaries for little recall.

8

No lidar ground truth worked around

ProblemOSDaR23's download server returned errors for days.

FixA calibration-free check with human height as the second ruler (section 5).

08 · Lessons, limitations, future work

What we would tell the next team

1
Evaluate on the deployment viewpoint.A test split drawn from the same source as the training data can hide a total failure on real footage.
2
Audit public data before training on it.Labels may not mean what the class name says, and published splits can leak.
3
Choose metrics for the downstream task.Track IoU could not tell two segmenters apart; the ranging deviation could.
4
Measure before optimising.Profiling found the real bottleneck; guesses would have optimised the wrong code.
5
Make long runs resumable.Checkpoints and retries turned every crash and restart into a few lost minutes.
6
Report negative results.Ideas that failed under measurement are part of the result and saved effort later.

Limitations

  • Distant objects (recall ≈ 0.25 for small objects): above ~70 km/h a person at the braking distance is too small for a single camera, so the system helps most at lower speeds, in yards, at stations and level crossings.
  • Ranging assumes locally flat track and a known or assumed focal length; error grows with distance and absolute accuracy was not checked against lidar.
  • Switches and merged tracks widen the corridor; rails embedded in road at level crossings are partly segmented as road; in darkness the unknown-object check switches off.
  • Small or weakly labelled evaluation sets: 268 Roboflow test images; RailSem19 boxes come from weakly supervised masks.

Future work

  • Validate ranging against lidar (OSDaR23) and calibrate per camera.
  • Far-range detection: zoomed crops around the far end of the corridor, or a telephoto second camera.
  • Thermal / near-infrared input for night and tunnels; temporal models for the corridor at switches.
  • Fixed cameras at blind curves and level crossings that warn trains before the hazard is visible from the cab.
